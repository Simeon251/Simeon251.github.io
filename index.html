Research Focus

Grids are already highly automated, but much of that automation still relies on fixed rules designed for grids dominated by synchronous generators.

Learned control that stays safe

How can inverter-based resources and batteries be controlled with learned policies that respect stability guarantees and device current limits?

Predicting faults and degradation

How can sensing and machine learning predict faults and battery wear early enough to plan maintenance instead of reacting to failures?

Methods for data-scarce systems

How can these methods be trained and trusted where historical data is limited, as in many microgrids in East Africa?

Tracing battery materials through Rwanda’s economy

Graduate Research • CMU-Africa • Jan 2026 – Present
Supervised by Prof. Sarath Tennakoon and Prof. Barry Rawn

Part of a CEPREC project on end-of-life options for microgrid batteries. In Rwanda, battery material flows are not tracked, so nobody knows how much sits in first-life use, second-life storage or waste.

I built a first-principles material flow analysis (MFA) model in Python to trace materials from import to end of life. Because national data to validate it does not yet exist, I am collecting laboratory degradation data to ground its second-life assumptions.
