Research

Grids are already highly automated, but much of that automation still runs on fixed rules built for grids dominated by synchronous generators.

Learned control that stays safe

How can inverter-based resources and batteries be controlled with learned policies that respect stability guarantees and device current limits?

Predicting faults and degradation

How can sensing and machine learning predict faults and battery wear early enough to plan maintenance instead of reacting to failures?

Methods for data-scarce systems

How can these methods be trained and trusted where data is limited, as in the systems I have studied in Rwanda?

Tracing battery materials through Rwanda’s economy

Graduate research, CMU-Africa, Jan 2026 to present. With Prof. Sarath Tennakoon and Prof. Barry Rawn.

Part of a CEPREC project on end-of-life options for microgrid batteries. A review of recycling routes showed that in Rwanda, battery materials cannot be traced through the economy, so nobody knows how much sits in first-life use, second-life storage or waste.

I built a Python material flow analysis model from first principles to trace them from import to end of life. It shows tracing is structurally possible, but no national data yet exists to validate it, so I am collecting laboratory degradation data to ground its second-life assumptions.
